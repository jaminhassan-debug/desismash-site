Halal vs HMC: what halal burgers really mean

By DesiSmash by Chef Hass · Nov 2026 · 2 min read

Halal means permitted under Islamic law. HMC is the Halal Monitoring Committee, one of the UK bodies that certifies food as halal. A burger can be halal without the seller holding its own certificate, and a certificate does not answer every question, so it helps to know what to ask.

People often type "halal burgers Leicester" and "HMC burgers" in the same search. They are related ideas, but they are not the same thing. Here is the plain-English difference.

What does halal mean for a burger?

For meat, halal means the animal and the way it was slaughtered meet Islamic requirements. For the rest of the burger it also means no pork and no alcohol, and ingredients such as gelatine and some cheese rennets can matter to people who are careful.

What is HMC?

HMC stands for Halal Monitoring Committee. It is a UK organisation that inspects and certifies food businesses and suppliers as halal. The Halal Food Authority (HFA) is another UK certifier. Different certifiers can have different standards, and not everyone agrees on every detail, such as how animals are stunned. If a certain standard matters to you, ask the seller which certifier their supplier uses.

Halal versus certified

  • Halal: the meat and ingredients meet halal requirements.
  • Certified halal: an independent body has checked and issued a certificate for that business or supplier.

The two can overlap, but they are not identical. A small kitchen may buy halal meat from a halal supplier and not hold its own certificate.

Questions to ask any burger seller

  1. Is the beef and chicken halal, and who is the supplier?
  2. Does the seller hold its own certificate, or does its supplier?
  3. Is there any pork or alcohol in the kitchen, sauces or cooking?
  4. Is the bacon, cheese or sauce suitable for a halal diet?
